# USCALE: Unified Structural Embedding and Core-Aware Label Expansion Framework for Community Detection in Multilayer Networks
## Abstract
Community detection in multilayer networks is challenging because of inter-layer dependencies, structural heterogeneity, and ambiguous community boundaries, which can limit the effectiveness of methods relying primarily on local propagation or direct structural optimization. This paper presents USCALE, a unified framework for community detection in multilayer networks that integrates structural information, anchor-guided label expansion, and representation-based label refinement. The multilayer network is first transformed into a weighted single-layer graph, followed by the selection of anchor nodes using structural criteria and statistical distinctiveness. A guided and synchronous label propagation process is then initiated from the selected anchors. To resolve ambiguities arising from multi-labeled nodes, node representations are learned by combining random walks, Word2Vec, and an AutoEncoder, and are subsequently used for representation-based community assignment. Finally, a global optimization step refines the resulting community structure to improve structural coherence. Extensive experiments on real-world and synthetic datasets demonstrate that USCALE accurately reconstructs communities with clear structures and achieves competitive performance in terms of ARI, NMI, FMI, and Purity. On networks with high mixing or ambiguous community boundaries, USCALE preserves meaningful structural patterns while balancing internal cohesion and alignment with reference labels, thereby reducing excessive community merging and over-segmentation. Overall, the results demonstrate that USCALE provides a stable and scalable framework for community detection across diverse multilayer network structures.
